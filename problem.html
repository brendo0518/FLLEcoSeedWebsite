The Problem

Biodiversity Is Under Threat

Why native habitats need restoring, and why that work is not always easy.

Understanding the threat

Three ways habitat goes wrong.

Habitat loss

Native vegetation is removed for farming, building, mining or other uses. Species that lived there lose their home.

Habitat fragmentation

What remains is split into smaller patches. Animals, pollen and seeds find it harder to move between them.

Degradation

Habitat that is still standing can decline through weeds, erosion, changed water flow, disease or altered fire patterns.

Western Australia

A place with a lot to lose.

The south-west of Western Australia is widely recognised as a global biodiversity hotspot, with many native plants that grow nowhere else. That is part of why we chose to focus here.

Why it matters

Biodiversity, native plants and pollinators.

Why biodiversity matters

A mix of plants, animals and other organisms helps ecosystems stay healthy and recover from stress. People depend on those systems for clean water, soil and shelter, and many people value nature for its own sake.

Why native plants matter

Native plants have grown with local soils, climate and animals. Many native animals rely on particular native plants for food and shelter, so choosing the right local species matters.

Why pollinators matter

Insects, birds and other animals carry pollen between flowers, which many plants need to reproduce. Restoring flowering native plants can support them in turn.

Why some sites are hard to reach

Steep slopes, dense scrub, wet ground, remote locations or fragile soils can make it hard, slow or unsafe for crews to walk in and plant by hand.
